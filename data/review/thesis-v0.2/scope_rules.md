# Scope rules for the thesis evaluation set (final, 2026-11-25)

Decided by the author before final adjudication. Candidates were reviewed with the question and options only; answer keys were not shown.

## Core rule

A question is in scope if its main tested concept is cardiovascular. A cardiac history, vital-sign value, risk factor, or cardiovascular distractor option is not sufficient on its own.

## Cardiovascular pharmacology

Include drug selection, treatment mechanisms, contraindications and adverse effects when the question directly tests cardiovascular care or physiology. Exclude primarily noncardiovascular drug effects and toxicology.

## Calculations

Include if the calculation directly computes a cardiovascular physiological parameter (e.g., cardiac output, ejection fraction, SVR, MAP).

## Vascular disease

Include aortic, peripheral arterial and venous thrombotic disease, and questions that directly test a circulatory or hemodynamic mechanism.

## Pulmonary embolism

Include pulmonary embolism and pulmonary vascular disease when they are the primary target of diagnosis, mechanism or management.

## Secondary neurological injury

Include if the primary tested concept is cardiovascular prevention or management of the cardiac source (e.g., anticoagulation for AF to prevent cardioembolic stroke); exclude pure neurological localization.

## Congenital heart disease in syndromes

Include diagnosis, embryology or etiology of a cardiac lesion. Exclude syndrome recognition and questions asking for a noncardiac associated finding.

## Other boundary cases

Assess scope and test eligibility separately. Keep exhibits described in sufficient text. Exclude essential missing exhibits and duplicates. Use unclear for unresolved scope, exhibit dependence or source quality.

## Labeling protocol

1. Three LLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) independently drafted labels for the 51 candidates, each under a different draft of the scope rules.
2. Agreement on `include_for_evaluation` across the three drafts: 36/51 (70.6%). Pairwise agreement: ChatGPT–Gemini 39/51, ChatGPT–Claude 39/51, Gemini–Claude 41/51 (different rule drafts; not inter-annotator agreement between humans).
3. The author fixed the final rules above; all candidates were then re-labeled under the final rules and the author confirmed every decision.
4. Reasons record the tested concept only; they do not state the answer.
